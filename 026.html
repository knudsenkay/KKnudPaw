Springer Spaniel Temperament

Springer Spaniel

The Springer Spaniel is energetic, athletic, and highly trainable -- perfect for active families.

🧡 Core Temperament Traits

Springer Spaniels are known for being:

  • Friendly & outgoing -- They greet life (and people) with enthusiasm.
  • Energetic & athletic -- Much higher drive than Cockers, Cavaliers, or Clumbers.
  • Intelligent & eager to work -- They thrive when given tasks, training, or jobs.
  • Affectionate & loyal -- They bond deeply with their families.
  • Alert & responsive -- They notice everything and react quickly.

They're often described as cheerful, bouncy, and eager.

🧒 Around Kids & Families

Springers are typically excellent family dogs:

  • Playful and fun for active kids
  • Gentle when properly trained
  • Thrive in busy, engaged households
  • Need consistent boundaries because their enthusiasm can become overwhelming

They're loving companions but not as mellow as Cavaliers or Clumbers.

🐾 With Other Dogs & Pets

  • Generally friendly and social
  • Can be competitive or assertive if not well‑socialised
  • Strong prey drive -- more likely to chase birds, rabbits, or fast‑moving animals
  • Usually good with other dogs, but their energy can annoy calmer breeds

They do best with other active pets.

🧠 Trainability & Intelligence

Springers are highly trainable:

  • Quick learners
  • Love having a job (retrieving, agility, obedience, scent work)
  • Respond well to positive reinforcement
  • Can become anxious or destructive if under‑stimulated
  • Need mental challenges as much as physical exercise

They're one of the most versatile working spaniels.

⚡ Energy Level & Exercise Needs

Springers are high‑energy:

  • 60–90 minutes of daily exercise
  • Off‑leash running or structured play
  • Mental stimulation (training, puzzles, scent games)
  • They excel in sports like agility, flyball, and field work

They're not a couch‑potato breed.

🏡 Ideal Home Type

A Springer Spaniel thrives in:

  • Active households
  • Families who enjoy outdoor activities
  • Homes with time for training and engagement
  • Environments where they aren't left alone for long periods

They're happiest when they're busy and included.
